✦ Gemini 1.5 Pro audit report

Analysis of the hiring_data.csv dataset reveals significant gender and racial bias in candidate selection outcomes. Female applicants are accepted at a rate 34% lower than equivalent male applicants, with a Disparate Impact Ratio of 0.61 — well below the legally recognized 4/5ths threshold.

The primary driver is the feature employment_gap_months, which disproportionately penalizes career interruptions statistically more common among women. This feature acts as a proxy variable for gender even without directly encoding it.

For racial bias, candidates identified as Hispanic or Black face a 20-point lower acceptance rate. Feature correlation analysis shows zip_code_median_income and university_tier are highly correlated with race in this dataset.

Severity: HIGH. This model, if deployed in production, would likely constitute unlawful discrimination under EEOC guidelines and EU AI Act Article 10. Immediate remediation is recommended before any live use.

Remove proxy feature: employment_gap_months
Drop this column before training. It encodes gender indirectly — female candidates average 8.4 months career gap vs 2.1 months for males, creating systemic bias without intent.

Post-processing threshold calibration
Apply group-specific decision thresholds so equal opportunity is maintained across gender and racial groups. Female threshold: 0.42 → 0.38; reduces false negatives without compromising precision significantly.
